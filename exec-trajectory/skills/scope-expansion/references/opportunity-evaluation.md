# Opportunity evaluation

## Matrix

Score each candidate 1-3 on each row, with a one-line reason. Use the scores to compare candidates; a 1 on legitimacy kills a candidate regardless of the total.

| Criterion | 3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise value | Moves a company top-3 priority, revenue, margin, or a material risk | Nice to have; nobody above the manager would notice |
| Legitimacy | Unowned, or the owner wants to hand it off, and the decider can grant it | Owned by someone who'd be diminished; or nobody with authority would grant it |
| Evidence value | Demonstrates a readiness dimension rated `absent` or `partial` | Repeats what the user already has plenty of evidence for |
| Visibility | Deciders for the next step would see the outcome and know it's the user's | Invisible, or credit would land elsewhere |
| Capability fit | Stretch but deliverable | Beyond current capability with no support |
| Risk | Failure is survivable and instructive | Failure would be public and hard to recover from |
| Cost to current scope | Clear handoff plan; core stays healthy | Core would slip |
| Durability | Can be formalized within a quarter | Likely to stay informal indefinitely |

## Anti-empire test

Answer honestly before pursuing any candidate:

1. If I owned this, who would benefit most: the company, the customers, my manager, or me?
2. Is there a current owner? Have I asked them what they want?
3. Would I be comfortable if every affected peer knew exactly why I want this?
4. Am I proposing a structure whose main effect is more scope for me?
5. What am I willing to give up to take this on?

If any answer is uncomfortable, redesign the approach (partner instead of take; propose an owner who isn't you; solve it as a project and hand it back).

## Situation type (Watkins' STARS)

| Type | What an early win looks like |
|---|---|
| Start-up | A first customer, pilot, or proof point; assembling the team |
| Turnaround | Stopping the bleeding; one decisive fix; restoring credibility |
| Accelerated growth | Putting structure and process under something that works |
| Realignment | Making the case for change to people who don't see the need yet |
| Sustaining success | Not breaking it; finding the next growth vector |

## One-page ask

```
Proposal: I'd like to own <X>.

The problem, in the company's terms: <what it costs or what it's worth; who's affected; evidence>.
Why now: <the window or trigger>.
What I'd do in the first 60 days: <3-5 concrete steps, with the first visible result>.
What I'd need: <decision rights, people, budget, air cover>.
What I'd hand off to make room: <specific items and to whom>.
How we'd know it's working: <2-3 measures and when>.
```

Sequence: pre-wire with the manager, then any current or adjacent owner, then the decider. Never let the manager hear about it from their own boss.
