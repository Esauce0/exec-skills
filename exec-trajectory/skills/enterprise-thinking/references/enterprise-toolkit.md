# Enterprise toolkit

## 1. Business-acumen translation (Charan)

Run on any proposal. Answer only the rows that apply; leave the rest out of the final text.

| Variable | Question | AI-product example |
|---|---|---|
| Cash | When does money go out and come in? | Compute commitments, prepaid capacity, revenue share with model vendors |
| Margin | Effect on gross margin? | Inference cost per task; human review cost; pricing power |
| Velocity | How fast do assets turn? | Time to value for customers; iteration and eval cycle time; sales cycle |
| Return | Margin × velocity against the capital and team time consumed | Engineer-quarters vs incremental gross profit |
| Growth | Profitable, differentiated, durable, organic? | Retained usage vs AI tourists; discount-driven adoption |
| Customers | Why do they choose us over the alternative? Which segment? | Enterprise trust, workflow position, data access |
| Total business | Effect on other units' numbers | Cannibalization, support load, sales compensation |

Output: one paragraph the CFO would accept without rewriting.

## 2. P&L drill (McCord)

1. Draw the company's P&L from memory. Mark where your product appears.
2. State your product's unit economics (for AI: cost per task, margin contribution, pricing logic).
3. Name the three company-level levers the CEO talks about most. Which does your roadmap move?
4. What should you do this week to move the number leadership watches?

Every gap becomes an assignment: a 1:1 with a finance partner, reading the last board deck or earnings call, sitting in on a sales forecast review.

## 3. Kernel test and bad-strategy screen (Rumelt)

| Element | Passes when | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis | Names the challenge and why it's hard, in two sentences | Restates a goal or lists trends |
| Guiding policy | Makes a choice that rules options out | "Be customer-centric," "win in AI" |
| Coherent actions | Commit resources; reinforce each other | A list of unrelated initiatives |

Screen: Is the challenge ever stated? Are the "strategies" really goals? Are there more than five unprioritized objectives? Does any sentence fail the plain-words test (translate it into plain words; if the translation is obvious or changes nothing tomorrow, it's fluff)?

**Crux worksheet:** list challenges → score importance and addressability → pick the best combination → write the guiding policy → pick one near-term objective → name the signal that would falsify the diagnosis.

## 4. Cross-functional perspective prompts

Before any recommendation reaches executives, write one line for each:

- **CFO:** What does it cost, when does it pay back, what's the range?
- **Head of Sales:** Does it help close or renew? Does it create promises sales will have to keep?
- **CTO / Engineering:** What does it displace? What does it do to reliability and on-call?
- **Legal / Security / Privacy:** What new exposure? (For AI: data rights, model behavior, regulation.)
- **Customer Success / Support:** What does it do to ticket volume and customer trust?
- **CEO:** Does it advance the company's central story or distract from it?

The line that's hardest to write is where the proposal is weakest.

## 5. Execution linkage (Charan, Execution)

A strategy isn't enterprise-grade until it answers:

- **People:** a named owner for each critical piece; are they capable; what if they leave?
- **Strategy:** explicit assumptions; milestones; a worked-out "how" to go with the "what."
- **Operations:** quarterly targets; synchronization across functions; contingencies.
- **Follow-through:** which recurring forum enforces it, and does it end in decisions?

## 6. Binding-constraint check

| Constraint | What executives are asking about | Proposals that help | Proposals that compete |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | Pipeline, win rate, expansion | New segments, conversion | Long platform investments |
| Margin | Gross margin, cost to serve | Efficiency, pricing | AI features with high inference cost |
| Cash | Burn, runway | Revenue pull-forward, cost cuts | Anything with long payback |
| Product gap | Losing deals on capability | Closing the gap | Adjacent bets |
| Trust / risk | Incidents, compliance, churn after incidents | Reliability, governance | Speed at the cost of quality |
| Talent | Hiring, attrition in key roles | Leverage, simplification | Anything needing hires the company can't make |
