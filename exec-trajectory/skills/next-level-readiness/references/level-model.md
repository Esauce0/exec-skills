# Level model

The company's own ladder always wins. Use this when `self/level-model.md` is empty, and as a sanity check when it isn't.

## Passage map for a product organization (after Charan)

Place people by the work; the titles below are only approximate. Three tests: what do their direct reports do (nothing, ICs, managers, whole functions); do they own economics (a P&L, a budget, a revenue line); are their peers in the same function or in other functions?

| Passage | Typical product titles (approximate) | Defining work | Time shift | Values shift | Signature failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Managing self | PM, Senior PM; Staff/Principal PM on a specialist track | Own an area; deliver at quality | Plan own work | Standards, collaboration | n/a |
| Managing others | Group PM, PM manager, small-team Director | Plan work, hire, assign, coach, measure | From doing to enabling | Making others productive over producing | Still writes the specs and prompts; team used as extra hands |
| Managing managers | Director, Senior Director | Select and develop managers; think across the business | Divest individual tasks | Developing managers; strategy and culture | Judges managers on product output; can't tell "can do" from "can lead" |
| Functional manager | VP Product, Head of Product | Long-horizon functional strategy; business team with Eng, Design, GTM heads | Business-team time over function time | Every sub-function; business need over turf | Protects turf; mistakes the roadmap for strategy |
| Business manager | CPO with P&L, GM | Profit lens across horizons; integrate functions | Reserved thinking time | The business over any function | Solves problems personally; functional team at war |
| Group / enterprise | EVP, President, CEO | Capital allocation across businesses; direction; culture; external constituencies | Long horizon plus quarterly mechanisms | Other leaders' businesses succeeding | Imposes own strategy; craves personal credit |

AI orgs often have Director or Principal titles with no reports. For those, use a specialist-pipeline lens: scope, ambiguity, cross-org influence, and business impact, rather than people leadership. Be explicit about which pipeline the user is on.

## Dimensions by level

Observable expectations. "Senior PM" is the baseline the user is growing from.

### 1. Scope and ambiguity (`scope-ambiguity`)
- **Senior PM:** owns a product or major area; problems mostly defined for them; turns fuzzy problems into testable hypotheses.
- **Director:** owns an area of several teams or products; handed undefined problems ("figure out what we do about X") and returns a framed problem, strategy, and plan.
- **VP:** decides which problems the organization works on, including white space no function owns; absorbs ambiguity so teams don't churn.
- **CPO/SVP:** sets part of the company's direction under deep uncertainty with the CEO and board.

### 2. Strategic contribution (`strategic-contribution`)
- **Senior PM:** a strong strategy for their own product.
- **Director:** strategy for an area that several teams act on; strategic context others cite; explicit about what won't be done.
- **VP:** product strategy for the function, tied to company strategy; shapes company strategy in executive forums; allocates across a portfolio.
- **CPO:** co-authors company strategy; represents it to the board and market.

### 3. Leverage through others (`leverage-through-others`)
- **Senior PM:** influences their own team; mentors informally; know-how influence on neighbors.
- **Director:** output through several PMs or managers; teams make good calls without them; written coaching plans.
- **VP:** output through leaders; builds the leadership bench; designs the operating system others run.
- **CPO:** through an executive team; designs the organization.

### 4. Judgment and decision altitude (`judgment`)
- **Senior PM:** sound product decisions with evidence; recommends rather than presenting options.
- **Director:** tradeoffs across products and with other functions; decides within the area; escalates only true cross-org calls, with a recommendation; speed calibrated to reversibility.
- **VP:** allocates resources across the function; makes one-way-door calls with peers and CEO; calibrated confidence.
- **CPO:** company-level bets and capital allocation.

### 5. Enterprise and business acumen (`enterprise-acumen`)
- **Senior PM:** knows their product's metrics and how it makes money.
- **Director:** frames bets in revenue, margin, cost, and risk; understands other functions' constraints; can explain the company's economics.
- **VP:** argues headcount and investment in business terms; values all functions; P&L literacy.
- **CPO:** profit lens across short and long horizons; balances the portfolio.

### 6. Talent and team building (`talent`)
- **Senior PM:** helps hire; mentors.
- **Director:** hires and develops PMs (and managers); judges people well; makes hard people calls; exports talent to other teams.
- **VP:** builds the leadership team; succession; talent density.
- **CPO:** executive hiring; culture.

### 7. Peer leadership and influence (`peer-leadership`)
- **Senior PM:** strong partner inside the product trio.
- **Director:** peers across functions treat them as a partner; resolves conflict at their level; escalates jointly; starts treating the peer group as a first team.
- **VP:** member of the leadership team whose first loyalty is that team; argues for company outcomes over function.
- **CPO:** leads within the executive team; board relationships.

### 8. Executive communication (`executive-communication`)
- **Senior PM:** clear, recommendation-first product updates.
- **Director:** writes at executive altitude; documents travel without them; handles executive challenge; pre-wires.
- **VP:** communicates through layers; represents the function to the CEO and board; narrative that shapes the organization.
- **CPO:** board, investors, market.

### 9. Operating rhythm (`operating-rhythm`)
- **Senior PM:** runs team rituals well.
- **Director:** designs the area's cadence (reviews, planning, metrics) so problems surface early.
- **VP:** the function's operating system and planning process.
- **CPO:** the company's product operating model.

### 10. Ownership of outcomes (`ownership`)
- **Senior PM:** owns their product's outcomes.
- **Director:** owns the area's outcomes, including other people's failures; "the miss is mine."
- **VP:** owns the function's outcomes publicly.
- **CPO:** owns the company's product outcomes.
