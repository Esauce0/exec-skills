# Promotion evidence

## The evidence standard

A piece of evidence counts toward a next-level case when it is:

| Test | Question | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Next-level | Does it show work the target level requires (per the level model)? | It's a great launch the user would have done at their current level anyway |
| Sustained | Has the user done this across at least two quarters? | One heroic quarter |
| Material | Did it matter to the business, in terms leadership uses? | Internal process improvement nobody above the manager cares about |
| Attributable | Is the user's specific contribution clear? | "The team" did it and the user's part is vague |
| Seen | Has someone in the promotion decision seen it directly? | Only the manager knows |
| Corroborated | Would someone other than the user describe it the same way? | Only the user's self-review says so |

Two especially strong kinds:
- **Barrel evidence** [Rabois]: the user took something from idea to done, recruiting others along the way, without needing to be managed.
- **White-box evidence** [Horowitz]: the user knew what to do in an ambiguous situation and got the organization to do it, which says more than a hit number does.

## The skeptic's questions

Run these as the strongest fair member of the promotion committee would. For each, decide whether the objection is right.

1. Is this person already doing the next-level job? For how long, and who has seen it?
2. What's the evidence beyond their manager's advocacy?
3. Where did they struggle, and how did they recover?
4. Can they lead people who know more than they do about the work?
5. Would the people already at the next level accept them as a peer?
6. Is there a role? (Headcount, budget, org design. A case with no role is a case for later.)
7. Are we rewarding past performance or predicting future performance?
8. What happens if we don't promote them? ("They might leave" belongs in a retention conversation.)
9. How do their peers across functions describe working with them?
10. What's the one thing that would worry us about them at the next level?

## Structuring the case (once the evidence exists)

1. **The claim:** "Operating at Director level on dimensions X, Y, Z since <quarter>."
2. **Three to five pieces of evidence,** each passing the standard above, each with the business outcome and who saw it.
3. **The development area,** named honestly, with what the user is doing about it. A case with no weakness reads as unexamined.
4. **Corroboration:** who else would say this (peers, cross-functional leaders, skip-level).
5. **Forward-looking:** what the user would do in the role in the first two quarters.

Use `executive-communication` to write it; the case should read answer-first, specific, and free of self-congratulation. Company outcomes first, with named credit to others (Horowitz: be ambitious for the company).

## When the evidence doesn't exist

Say so, then turn each missing piece into a leap assignment with a date, and agree the criteria with the manager (and ideally a sponsor) so the goalposts don't move. Record them in `commitments.md`.
