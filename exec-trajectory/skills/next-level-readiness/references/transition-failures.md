# Transition failure modes

## By passage (Charan)

| Transition | Values the person must adopt | Failure that shows up |
|---|---|---|
| PM to managing others | Satisfaction from others' output | Keeps writing the specs, running evals personally, being the sharpest voice in the review |
| To managing managers (Director) | Developing managers; judging them on their leadership | Judges managers on product output; hoards the interesting problems |
| To functional manager (VP) | Valuing every sub-function; business need over turf | Over-directs PM craft; neglects design, research, data, product ops; fights for headcount on functional grounds |
| To business manager | Profit over any function; protected thinking time | Solves problems personally; tolerates a functional team at war |

**Values probe:** "Name the three things you're proudest of this quarter. Whose work were they? What did only you contribute?" If all three are the user's own craft work, the values shift hasn't happened.

## The seven shifts as behaviors (Watkins)

| Shift | Behavior you can observe | How to practice below the title |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist to generalist | Evaluates and decides in functions they've never run | Draft what "great" looks like for Eng, Design, Data, PMM, Sales leadership; trade insight with peers there |
| Analyst to integrator | Makes and explains cross-functional tradeoffs | Write tradeoff memos that say what's given up and why |
| Tactician to strategist | Shifts between detail and big picture; anticipates reactions | Before a launch, simulate how a competitor, regulator, and large customer respond |
| Bricklayer to architect | Treats the org as a system of structure, process, incentives | Diagnose alignment before proposing any reorg |
| Problem solver to agenda setter | Decides which problems the org works on | Name three issues the org should tackle in 12 months that nobody owns |
| Warrior to diplomat | Shapes the environment through negotiation and alliances | Lead a partner negotiation, an industry working group, or an enterprise customer's risk review |
| Supporting cast to lead role | Knows their words get amplified; communicates through layers | Check how your message reaches people two levels down; build one unfiltered channel to the front line |

## Habits that get worse with seniority (Goldsmith)

Most are strengths overused. Screen the user's recent meetings and messages for:

winning too much; adding too much value; passing judgment; destructive comments; starting with "no," "but," or "however"; telling the world how smart we are; speaking when angry; negativity ("let me explain why that won't work"); withholding information; failing to give recognition; claiming credit you don't deserve; making excuses; clinging to the past; playing favorites; refusing to express regret; not listening; failing to express gratitude; punishing the messenger; passing the buck; an excessive need to be "me"; and goal obsession.

Adding too much value deserves special attention for PMs. A senior person's "small improvement" to a team's idea may make it 5% better and cut the team's commitment by far more. The cost rises with rank.

Goldsmith's point about perception matters for the observer: people's perception lags behind a changed behavior, so the change alone won't register. His process: acknowledge it, tell people what you're changing, follow up regularly, and ask for suggestions about the future (feedforward) with only "thank you" in reply.

## Misconceptions of new leaders (Hill)

| Expectation | Reality |
|---|---|
| Authority comes with the role | The job is interdependence; you depend on many people you don't control |
| Position confers power | Power comes from credibility; showing off expertise can undermine it |
| Control gets results | Control gets compliance; leaders need commitment |
| Lead through one-on-one relationships | You have to lead the team as a team |
| The job is keeping things running | The job includes driving change, including outside your formal authority |

## Identity lag (Doshi; Ibarra)

- **"Prove I still have it":** the new leader rewrites PRDs, dominates reviews, jumps into execution. Measure the leader by the quality of questions they ask in their team's reviews.
- **Competency trap:** comfort work crowds out leadership work. Substitute outward-facing activities; don't just block calendar time.
- **Authenticity trap:** "that's not me" about influence, self-advocacy, or vision-setting. Treat it as a hypothesis to test with a low-stakes experiment.
- **Expect regression** under stress. Judge the trajectory over quarters, not weeks.
