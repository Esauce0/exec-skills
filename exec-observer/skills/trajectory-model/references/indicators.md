# Indicators

## Leading indicators

| Indicator | What it looks like | Why it leads | Innocent explanation to rule out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undefined problems handed over | "Can you figure out what we should do about X?" with no spec | Delegating ambiguity is the highest-trust allocation short of people and money | The delegator is overloaded and dumping; check whether it's important to them |
| Asked for views outside scope | An exec asks your opinion on another team's plan, a hire, a strategy | They value your judgment beyond your execution | They needed a quick technical read (expert posture) |
| New rooms | Invited to staff, planning, reviews, exec offsites, customer escalations | Rooms are scarce; inviting you costs the host | A one-off need for your content; check if the invite recurs |
| Dropped from rooms | Meetings you used to attend continue without you | Often the first visible sign of a trust or relevance drop | Meeting restructured; calendar hygiene; check who else was dropped |
| Framing reuse | Your terms, frameworks, or diagnosis appear in others' docs and exec updates | Your thinking is shaping the organization's | Credit may not travel with the framing; check attribution |
| Named as owner by others | Peers or execs say "X owns that" without your manager prompting | Organizational recognition of scope | They're pointing at you to offload a problem |
| Advocacy above the manager | A skip-level or exec mentions you for something, defends your work, or puts you forward | Sponsorship forming | Reported second-hand; verify with a direct signal |
| Informal becoming formal | Scope you already run appears in a plan, OKR, or announcement | Durable scope; survives reorgs | None; this is almost always real |
| Escalation pattern changes | People start bringing you problems for you to decide | Others treat you as a decision-maker | You're the only one who knows the system |

## Lagging indicators

Title, level, compensation, formal headcount, and performance rating confirm what already happened, so they help calibrate the model but can't steer anything week to week.

## Drift signals in detail

| Signal | How to detect | Innocent explanation | Discriminating check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Praise without allocation | LOG: many `recognition`/`feedback` entries, few `allocation` | Allocation frozen company-wide (budget, hiring freeze) | Are peers getting scope while you get praise? |
| Visibility concentrated | Visibility map: only the manager has direct exposure | New in role; manager protects the channel on purpose | Has the manager ever offered to bring you to their boss's forums? |
| Load without altitude | Scope ledger: people/products up, ambiguity and strategic importance flat | The company needs execution now | Are the undefined problems going to someone else? |
| Repeated "not yet" | Feedback entries across cycles with the same content | The bar is genuinely high and you're closing the gap | Is the content of "not yet" changing? If not, it's a pattern |
| Area drifting from priorities | Product strategy vs company top 3 | Temporary; strategy cycles | Is headcount or attention moving away from your area? |
| Sponsor weakening | Sponsor's own scope or standing shrinking; new boss above them | Normal churn | Do you have a second sponsor outside that chain? |
| Consulted less or later | Decisions in your area made before you hear | Fast-moving period | Is it one person or several? Did something precede it? |
| Grade A dominance | Monthly grade mix mostly A | The role offers no B opportunities | That's an opportunity gap; see scope-expansion |

## Rate

A trajectory has a speed as well as a direction, so compare scope ledger snapshots quarter to quarter. When `self/level-model.md` has revealed-ladder data (how long recent promotees took, what they did), compare the user's rate to that base rate rather than to their hopes.
