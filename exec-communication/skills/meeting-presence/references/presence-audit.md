# Presence audit

## Gravitas behaviors (after Hewlett)

Score the user's last two or three high-stakes moments, each with an observable example. Use the user's account plus at least one senior observer's where possible.

| Behavior | Observable example | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Composure under challenge | How they responded the last time an executive attacked their numbers | |
| Decisiveness | Made a call or clear recommendation rather than listing options | |
| Speaking truth upward | Disagreed with a senior person, with evidence, in a room that mattered | |
| Reading the room | Adjusted when the room's energy or concern shifted | |
| Reputation | Others cite the user's track record unprompted | |
| Vision | Articulated where something should go, beyond the data | |

Communication: clear and concise; held the room; adapted in real time.
Appearance and filter: nothing distracted from the content, including video setup.
Blunder check: any flip-flop, loss of composure, overclaim, careless remark.

## Habits checklist (after Goldsmith)

Mark any observed in the last month, with the moment:

- Adding too much value (improving others' ideas in ways that cost their ownership)
- Winning too much (needing to win minor points)
- Starting with "no," "but," or "however"
- Telling the room how smart we are (showing expertise instead of using it)
- Not listening (waiting to talk; interrupting)
- Passing judgment on others' ideas reflexively
- Making excuses when challenged
- Failing to give credit in the room
- Speaking when angry

## Challenge-handling patterns

| Challenge | Weak response | Strong response |
|---|---|---|
| "Your number looks wrong." | Defend the methodology at length | "Could be. Here's how we got it; if you're seeing something different I'll reconcile by tomorrow." |
| "Why not just use <off-the-shelf thing>?" | Argue the specific suggestion | Find the concern: "Is the worry the timeline? If so, here's how we'd shorten it." |
| "I'm not convinced." | Repeat the case louder | "What would convince you?" Then either answer or agree on the test. |
| A question you can't answer | Guess | "I don't know. I'll have it by Thursday." |
| Executive redirects the meeting | Try to get back to your agenda | Follow them; find where your point fits their question |
| Decision goes against you | Relitigate | State the concern once if not yet heard; commit; move on |

## Feedback triage for presence comments (after Hewlett, Ibarra)

For each piece of presence feedback:
1. Which behavior, in which moment? If the giver can't say, ask.
2. Capability signal (composure, decisiveness, clarity, listening) or conformity signal (accent, energy, dress, style)?
3. Swap test (after Scott): would the same behavior from someone of a different gender or background get the same label?
4. What does adapting cost the user?
5. Decide: adapt, experiment with it in a low-stakes setting, or decline and compensate elsewhere.

## Meeting-purpose check (after Scott's Get Stuff Done wheel)

Before the meeting, name the stage: listen, clarify, debate, decide, persuade, implement, learn. PMs commonly skip "clarify" (a half-formed idea gets shot down in a big forum) and "persuade" (assuming a decision carries itself to people who weren't there).
