# Product leadership audits

Operational tools distilled mainly from Cagan (Inspired, Empowered, Transformed, SVPG), with Doshi, Rumelt, and Horowitz where noted. Run them against the user's real teams and artifacts, from the Product Brains where possible.

## 1. Team-type diagnostic

Run per team the user leads or influences.

| Question | Delivery team | Feature team | Empowered product team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who decides what gets built? | Executives | Stakeholders, via the roadmap | The team, against an assigned problem |
| What is it measured on? | Output shipped | Features shipped on date | An outcome metric moved |
| When a launch misses the metric? | Nothing happens | Team blamed; stakeholder moves on | Team iterates; it owns the result |
| PM's main activity | Backlog administration | Project management, stakeholder wrangling | Discovery on value and viability |

Advice follows the column. In a feature-team company, the credibility move is to win one problem-shaped objective and report the outcome.

## 3. Strategy check

1. **Focus:** how many objectives are "top priority"? More than three or four fails.
2. **Insight:** for each, what data, customer learning, technology shift, or market change makes it pivotal? "A stakeholder asked" and "a competitor did it" are not insights.
3. **Action:** which team owns it, with what objective, and what context have they been given?
4. **Management:** what weekly mechanism surfaces stalls? What obstacle did the leader remove last month?

Rumelt stress test: is there a *diagnosis* that names the challenge? A *guiding policy* that says how we'll deal with it (and what we won't do)? *Coherent actions* that reinforce each other? Goals dressed as strategy ("grow AI adoption 3x") fail the first test.

## 4. Leader time audit

Classify a typical week for someone who leads people:

| Bucket | Hours | Target direction |
|---|---|---|
| Coaching 1:1s and written coaching plans | | Up |
| Staffing (hiring loops, recruiting, onboarding) | | Up when hiring |
| Strategy and context work (writing, reviewing, socializing) | | Up |
| Stakeholder previews and alignment | | Steady |
| Delivery and roadmap meetings | | Down |
| Doing PM work for a team member | | Near zero |

A people manager spending more on delivery meetings than on coaching and staffing is operating below the role.

## 5. PM coaching grid

Rate each PM (or the user, as a self-assessment) and write one dated action per gap: a resource, a relationship to build, or a practice assignment.

| Group | Areas |
|---|---|
| Product knowledge | Users and customers; data; industry and domain; the business (sales, marketing, finance, legal, partnerships) |
| Process skills | Discovery; optimization; delivery; the development process |
| People skills | Team collaboration; stakeholder management; evangelism; leadership |

## 6. Complaint-to-credibility conversion

| Complaint | Credibility action |
|---|---|
| "Execs just want dates." | Deliver one high-integrity commitment: time-boxed discovery, then a date, then hit it. |
| "We're not empowered." | Ask for one problem-shaped objective instead of a feature list; deliver an outcome; report it. |
| "My PMs are weak." | Write a coaching plan per PM with dated actions; review monthly. |
| "Engineering doesn't care about customers." | Put engineers in front of customers every week. |
| "Sales keeps making promises." | Preview solutions with sales leadership early, one-on-one, before they commit. |
| "Leadership keeps changing priorities." | Write the strategy that makes the priority stable, and get it agreed. |

## 7. Fake-execution-problem test (Doshi)

Before accepting a new sync, tracker, or review process as the fix:
1. Has a similar fix been tried? How long did it last?
2. Is there a written strategy both teams accept?
3. What does each team get rewarded for?
4. Which two people need a direct conversation?

If the fix failed before, the problem is strategy, incentives, or a relationship, and that is what to fix.
