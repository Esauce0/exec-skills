# Posture leaks and their fixes

Each leak is a recurring behavior pattern. The fix is behavioral; wording follows.

## Escalation leak
**Pattern:** sending decisions upward that fall within your authority.
**Why it costs:** it tells the executive you won't carry the weight of your own scope, and it spends their attention on your work.
**Fix:** before any escalation, sort each item: mine to decide; mine to recommend, theirs to decide; theirs to decide, I need to inform. Decide the first group and report the decisions in one line each. Only the second group becomes an ask.
**Test:** would your boss be surprised you asked? If yes, it was yours.

## Option-list leak
**Pattern:** presenting options with balanced pros and cons and no recommendation.
**Why it costs:** it hands the synthesis, the hard part, to a more senior person.
**Fix:** make a recommendation, state what it costs, and say what evidence would change your mind.

## Activity leak
**Pattern:** reporting meetings, syncs, effort.
**Why it costs:** it signals that you measure yourself by motion.
**Fix:** report state changes and implications. If nothing changed, say so in one line.

## Dependency leak
**Pattern:** "blocked by," "waiting on," "depends on X team."
**Why it costs:** it frames you as a passenger in your own project.
**Fix:** name the constraint, what you've done to move it, your fallback, and the trigger for escalating.

## Expert leak
**Pattern:** answering the technical question with precision and stopping there.
**Why it costs:** you become a consultant to decision-makers instead of one of them.
**Fix:** answer, then give the implication and your recommendation. "Yes, the model can do it at 94% accuracy. At that rate I'd ship it for internal use and hold it from customers until we hit 98%."

## Lane leak
**Pattern:** only raising issues inside your product in cross-functional forums.
**Why it costs:** organizational leaders are identified by caring about problems beyond their charter.
**Fix:** once a month, bring one cross-team problem you can see from your seat, with a proposed resolution and an offer to help.

## Warrior leak
**Pattern:** winning arguments with peers; escalating conflicts as complaints.
**Why it costs:** executives need leaders who resolve conflict at their level (Watkins: warrior to diplomat).
**Fix:** resolve directly with the peer; if you can't, escalate jointly with a shared statement of facts and each side's case.

## Credit leak
**Pattern:** first person for wins, "we" or passive for misses.
**Why it costs:** executives notice asymmetry immediately; it reads as low integrity.
**Fix:** "we" for team wins with specific credit to named people; "I" for misses you own.

## Theater leak
**Pattern:** senior vocabulary and urgency without decisions, numbers, or ownership.
**Why it costs:** it tells executives you're performing a level you haven't reached, which is worse than being plainly at your level.
**Fix:** remove the vocabulary and see what's left. Add the missing decision, cost, or owner. If there isn't one, narrow the claim.

## Over-correction (overreach)
**Pattern:** after coaching, the user starts deciding things outside their authority, speaking for their boss, or criticizing peers' areas in front of executives.
**Why it costs:** trust drops faster from overreach than it rises from initiative.
**Fix:** recommend at the next level, decide at your own. Pre-wire anything that touches a peer's or your boss's ownership before you raise it in a group.
