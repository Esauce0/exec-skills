# Posture ladder: observable markers

Markers are things a senior observer can see or hear. Use them to classify a specific artifact or interaction, never to label a person permanently.

## Coordinator

- **Writing:** "syncing," "aligning," "looping in," "waiting on." Updates list meetings held. Follow-ups relay what others said, with no view and no decision.
- **Meetings:** runs the agenda, takes notes, tracks action items; rarely states a view on the substance.
- **Escalation:** almost everything; any disagreement goes up.
- **Ambiguity:** waits for someone to define the task.
- **Signal to executives:** useful, replaceable, not an owner.

## Project manager

- **Writing:** milestones, status colors, dates, dependencies. Risks framed as schedule risk.
- **Meetings:** reports progress against plan; defends the timeline.
- **Escalation:** scope and resource conflicts.
- **Ambiguity:** asks for requirements; doesn't question whether the project should exist.
- **Signal:** reliable delivery; judgment about the *what* is untested.

## Subject-matter expert

- **Writing:** precise, technical, often long; qualifications and edge cases; "it depends."
- **Meetings:** answers questions well; corrects errors; rarely proposes direction.
- **Escalation:** rarely escalates; also rarely takes ownership of non-technical tradeoffs.
- **Ambiguity:** narrows it to the technically answerable part.
- **Signal:** valuable resource to consult. Risk: becoming the person others consult for answers while ownership goes elsewhere. For AI PMs this is the most common trap, because AI expertise is scarce and executives will happily keep consulting you forever.

## Product manager

- **Writing:** users, problems, outcomes, metrics for one product; tradeoffs within the product.
- **Meetings:** recommends product decisions with evidence; defends the problem as well as the plan.
- **Escalation:** cross-product tradeoffs and resourcing beyond their team.
- **Ambiguity:** turns a fuzzy problem into a testable hypothesis.
- **Signal:** owns a product's outcomes. The baseline for this user's current role.

## Product leader

- **Writing:** bets across a portfolio, strategic context for several teams, what we won't do, team health and capability.
- **Meetings:** raises the quality of other people's product decisions; asks the question that reframes the discussion; speaks for a whole area.
- **Escalation:** tradeoffs between product areas or with other functions' strategy.
- **Ambiguity:** creates strategic context that lets several teams act without asking.
- **Other people's behavior:** PMs outside their reporting line seek their judgment; their framing shows up in others' docs.
- **Signal:** can be trusted with an area and with other PMs.

## Organizational leader

- **Writing:** organization design, talent, operating cadence, cross-functional tradeoffs, resource allocation across teams.
- **Meetings:** treats the peer leadership group as the first team; argues for company outcomes even at a cost to their own area; proposes resolutions to cross-functional conflicts.
- **Escalation:** only what needs executive authority or crosses organizations; arrives with a joint recommendation from the peers involved.
- **Ambiguity:** absorbs it for the organization; shields teams from churn while making hard calls.
- **Other people's behavior:** executives delegate undefined problems to them; they are asked to fix things outside their formal area.
- **Signal:** can be trusted with people, budget, and an organization's results.

## Executive

- **Writing:** business outcomes (revenue, margin, risk, market position), explicit choices and their costs, what the company will not do.
- **Meetings:** allocates across competing priorities; makes the call when the room is split; owns outcomes for failures in their organization, publicly.
- **Escalation:** to the CEO or board only on company-level decisions, with a recommendation.
- **Ambiguity:** sets direction without complete information and adjusts deliberately.
- **Other people's behavior:** represents the company to the board, customers, and the market.
- **Signal:** can be trusted with a piece of the company's fate.

## Cross-cutting tells

| Tell | Usually indicates |
|---|---|
| "Let me know what you think" at the end of a recommendation | Permission-seeking; replace with a default action and a deadline to object |
| Three or more questions to the boss in one update | Escalation leak |
| Updates that would read the same if the business had a bad week | Activity reporting |
| Only raising issues inside own scope in cross-functional forums | Stuck at current posture in the room |
| Criticizing another team without a proposed resolution | Warrior behavior (Watkins' warrior-to-diplomat shift not yet made); reads below organizational leader |
| Deep technical answers to strategic questions | Expert posture in a leadership room |
| Default note-taker and recap-sender for other people's meetings | Coordinator role by default; fine occasionally, costly as identity. A follow-up on a decision you drove or an outcome you own sends the opposite signal |
| "My team" for everything, "the org" never | Ownership that stops at the edge of their own unit |
| First person only for wins, passive voice for misses | Low accountability signal |
