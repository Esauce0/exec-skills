# Bias checks for career self-assessment

Each bias below shows up predictably when someone assesses their own trajectory. Each has one counter-question. Ask it, and skip the lecture about the bias.

| Bias | How it shows up in career reasoning | Counter-question |
|---|---|---|
| Self-serving attribution | Wins are my judgment; misses were the reorg, the data team, the exec who changed their mind. | "If a peer had this exact outcome, what would you say caused it?" |
| Fundamental attribution error (about others) | The VP who pushed back is "political" or "doesn't get AI." | "What is she measured on this quarter, and how does your proposal affect that number?" |
| Spotlight effect | One awkward exchange in a meeting feels like it defined the executive's view of me. | "How many interactions has this person had with you in the last 90 days, and what did the others look like?" |
| Effort justification | This was hard and took months, so it must be promotion-worthy. | "What changed for the business because of it, and who with authority knows that?" |
| Recency bias | Last week's great review with the CPO outweighs a year of flat scope. | "What has been allocated to you (scope, people, budget, rooms) in the last two quarters?" |
| Resulting | The launch went well, so the call was right. The launch flopped, so the call was wrong. | "Given what you knew at the time, would you make the same call again? What would you do differently in the process?" |
| Halo from one sponsor | "My boss loves me" becomes "leadership sees me as ready." | "Who else above your boss has seen your work directly, and what did they do afterward?" |
| Title anchoring | Measuring readiness by comparison with the least impressive person at the next level. | "What does the strongest person at the next level do that you don't yet do?" |
| Competency trap (Ibarra) | More of what you're already great at feels like growth. | "What did you do this month that you couldn't have done a year ago, versus things you did better?" |
| Narrative lock-in | The user has a story ("I'm undervalued here") and every event gets fitted to it. | "What evidence in the last month doesn't fit that story?" |
| Over-ownership | Taking full blame for structural failures, which reads as low judgment about what was controllable. | "Which parts of this were in your control, which were in your influence, and which were neither?" |

## When to run them

- Any time the user brings a single event and asks what it means.
- Before any promotion or readiness verdict.
- In every monthly review, pick the two biases most active in the month's narrative and apply their counter-questions.
