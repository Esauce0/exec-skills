# Sponsor portfolio

Distilled from the sylvia-ann-hewlett and herminia-ibarra dossiers.

## Sponsor-or-mentor test (after Hewlett, 2010)

For each senior relationship, mark yes / no / unknown, with evidence.

Required for "sponsor":
- Has spent political capital for the user.
- Has advocated, or would advocate, for the user's next promotion.

Plus at least two of: expands what the user thinks they can do; connects them to senior leaders; connects them outside the company; promotes their visibility; connects them to opportunities; gives career advice; gives candid feedback on presence.

"Unknown" on either required item means not yet a sponsor.

Context worth knowing: in Hewlett's 2010 research, far more senior people described themselves as sponsors than reported actually spending political capital for anyone. Self-description is weak evidence in both directions.

## Portfolio map

| Candidate | Level vs user | Clout (talent review seat, budget, roles) | Line of sight to user's work | What they gain from the user | Distinct value user brings | Trust evidence | Ladder level | Last substantive contact | Next exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Target: two or three real sponsors across at least two power centers, one ideally outside the company. Flag any single point of failure.

## Protégé value audit (performance, trust, distinct value)

- **Performance:** outcomes this person personally observed in the last two quarters. Outcomes they only heard about count for half.
- **Trust:** no surprises; discretion kept; commitment after decisions; candor in private; feedback acted on and reported back.
- **Distinct value:** one sentence on what the user offers that this person lacks, and one instance where it helped them. For an AI PM this is often credible AI judgment the sponsor needs for their own agenda.

A weak leg is the next development priority.

## Cultivation cadence

1. Pick two potential sponsors from the map.
2. Find or create a real work reason to be in their line of sight: a review they chair, a problem they own.
3. Deliver a crisp result; follow up briefly, tied to their goals.
4. Ask for one specific piece of feedback; act on it; report back within weeks.
5. Keep recurring contact on the calendar, the way you would with a key client.
6. Once trust exists, make a specific ask (a stretch assignment, a nomination, "would you support me for X?") framed in their interest.
7. Reciprocate: take work off their plate; bring them information and your network.

## Spectrum movement (this system's adaptation of Ibarra)

Ibarra's spectrum runs mentor, strategizer, connector, opportunity giver, advocate. This system folds strategizer into mentor and calls the advocate level "sponsor," matching Hewlett's definition.

| From | To | What usually moves it |
|---|---|---|
| Mentor | Connector | They see you handle something well that matters to a peer of theirs |
| Connector | Opportunity-giver | A small assignment from them, delivered completely and fast |
| Opportunity-giver | Sponsor | A high-stakes result they can point to, plus knowing exactly what you want next |

A pipeline with only mentors is a warning sign.
