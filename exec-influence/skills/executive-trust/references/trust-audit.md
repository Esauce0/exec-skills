# Trust audit

## Per-executive audit (after Campbell)

Fill each row from dated events you can point to. "Unknown" is a valid answer and becomes an action.

| Component | Question | Evidence (dated) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Word | Every commitment to them in the last quarter kept, or renegotiated before it was missed? | | |
| Loyalty | What have I said about them to others when they weren't there? | | |
| Integrity | Any update where my reported status was rosier than what I knew? | | |
| Discretion | Have I repeated anything they said privately? | | |
| Candor | Have I told them something unwelcome, privately, with care? | | |
| Team-first | Have they seen me give something up for the larger goal? | | |

Any "no" or "unknown" becomes a concrete action before the next scope conversation.

## Trust-equation diagnosis (after Maister)

| Component | What weakness looks like | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Credibility (words) | They challenge your facts or check them elsewhere | Precision; say "I don't know, I'll have it by Thursday"; bring the number |
| Reliability (actions) | They add buffer to your dates; they chase your follow-ups | Keep small promises; flag slips before they happen |
| Intimacy (safety) | They don't bring you problems early; you hear about issues after they're public | Handle hard news calmly; keep confidences |
| Self-orientation | They discount recommendations that would benefit you | Recommend against your own interest when it's right; credit others specifically |

## Pledge check (after Cagan)

In the last quarter, with this executive:
- Did I show the reasoning behind each conclusion?
- Did I own the outcome, including misses?
- When I disagreed, did I bring evidence or a test result?
- Did I keep every commitment I made?
- Did anything I shipped surprise them?
- Did I protect focus, or add to the pile?

## Coachability check

- The last three pieces of critical feedback the user got: what changed, how fast, and did the giver see the change?
- Does the user's retelling of feedback start with justification ("they didn't have the context") before acknowledgment?
- Has the user asked anyone senior for feedback, unprompted, in the last month? (Scott's technique: ask a specific question about a recent event, tolerate the silence, listen to understand, then visibly act or explain why not.)

## Repair protocol (after a trust-damaging event)

1. **Own it, specifically and fast.** What happened, what your part was, what it cost them. No "mistakes were made."
2. **Say what changes,** concretely, not "I'll do better."
3. **Demonstrate over several interactions.** Trust returns slowly, through a run of evidence.
4. **Don't ask for anything** (scope, a role, a favor) during repair.
5. **Close the loop** after a few weeks: "Since the Q3 slip I've been re-baselining weekly; is that giving you what you need?"
6. Record the event and the repair steps as evidence entries; the observer should see the recovery as well as the damage.
